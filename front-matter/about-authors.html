About the Authors

Building Vision AI: From Pixels to Generative Models is written by Alexander Apartsin together with a 42-agent AI writing pipeline. The human author sets the book's vision, structure, scope, and quality standards, defines what each chapter must teach and how the four-part arc fits together, and directs and reviews the work; the pipeline, a coordinated team of specialized AI agents, drafts, critiques, revises, illustrates, and cross-checks the material under that direction. The result is a collaboration in the plainest sense: human editorial judgment about what matters and why, applied at every stage to machine-scale drafting and review.

How This Book Was Made

The pipeline behind this book is a staged production line of 42 specialized agents, each responsible for one aspect of quality. A chapter begins as an outline and passes through planning, content drafting, and structural review; dedicated agents then verify self-containment (no concept used before it is taught), audit clarity and engagement, check learning quality against the chapter's stated objectives, and enforce consistency of terminology, notation, and cross-references across all 39 chapters. Further stages handle the visual identity and diagrams, the epigraphs, the applied industry examples, the exercises, and the bibliographies (with every reference checked against a real, reachable source), followed by a final polish, a quality challenge that deliberately attacks each chapter's weakest points, and a meta-review before the chapter is integrated into the book.

This way of working has a practical consequence for you as a reader: the book's signature consistency, the recurring callout system, the paired from-scratch and library implementations, the cross-reference arcs that connect convolution to CNNs and denoising to diffusion, is not a style guide that authors tried to remember. It is enforced mechanically, chapter after chapter, by agents whose only job is to check it.

It also has a consequence worth stating plainly. A book produced this way is reviewed many more times than any single human could manage, and still, errors can survive any process. Where the text and reality disagree, reality wins; the errata for this edition will be maintained alongside the book, and corrections are folded into subsequent printings of the text.
