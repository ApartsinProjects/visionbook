## P1: Missing frontier topics (important, not covered)

| Chapter | Missing topic | Why important | Where to add |
|---|---|---|---|
| 27 (depth) | Video Depth Anything (CVPR 2025 Highlight) | The book covers Depth Anything V2 for single images but does not mention Video Depth Anything, which adds temporally consistent dense depth for arbitrarily long videos using a novel Temporal Gradient Matching loss. It is the de-facto standard for video depth as of 2025 and fills an explicit gap between the monocular depth section and the video section. | Section 27.1 or a new frontier callout in section 27.6 |
| 27 (3D) | 4D Gaussian Splatting for dynamic scenes (mainstream 2024-2025) | The Research Frontier callout in section 27.5 mentions "Dynamic splatting (4D Gaussian Splatting, Deformable 3DGS, 2024)" but in one sentence. By 2025-2026 this is an active sub-field with dedicated language-guided editing (Ctrl-D at CVPR 2025, EditSplat at CVPR 2025, 3DITScene at ICLR 2025). The book has no coverage of the editing side. | Extend section 27.5 frontier callout or add to section 36.4 generative rendering |
| 37 (evaluation) | VQAScore and GenAI-Bench | Section 37.1 covers FID, KID, Precision-Recall, and CLIPScore but does not mention VQAScore (Li et al., ECCV 2024), which is now the standard text-image alignment metric (adopted by Google Imagen 3/4, Bytedance Seed, NVIDIA) and is empirically stronger than CLIPScore at measuring compositional faithfulness. GenAI-Bench (CVPR 2024 Workshop) provides the companion compositional-prompt benchmark suite. | Section 37.1, after CLIPScore subsection |
| 23 (detection) | YOLO26 (Jan 2026) and YOLO12 (Feb 2025) in code examples | The prose in 23.3 already acknowledges "the YOLO12 line in 2025" but all runnable code still uses `yolo11n.pt`. A reader following the code examples is one-to-two versions behind. | Section 23.6 code snippets and section 28 deployment examples |
| 25 (foundation models) | SigLIP 2 (Google DeepMind, Feb 2025) | The book mentions SigLIP and has a bibliography entry for SigLIP 2 in the chapter 34 index, but section 25.6 foundation-model landscape table lists only "CLIP / SigLIP" without acknowledging SigLIP 2's material improvements: multi-resolution training, self-distillation, masked prediction, and significantly better dense-prediction and multilingual performance. SigLIP 2 has become a common drop-in replacement encoder in the VLM community. | Section 25.6 Table 25.6.1 and section 34.1 encoder discussion |
| 25/28 (VLMs) | Modern VLM generation: Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3-VL, InternVL3 | Section 25.6 covers the VLM template and mentions LLaVA / LLaVA-1.5 and BLIP-2 / Flamingo as examples. But by 2025-2026 the leading open-weights VLMs are Qwen3-VL (72B, Oct 2025) and InternVL3.5 (Aug 2025), which use reinforcement learning post-training and achieve scores far above LLaVA-1.5. The book's VLM examples feel circa-2023. | Section 25.6 subsection on generative VLMs; add a frontier callout naming Qwen3-VL and InternVL3 |
| 36 (video gen) | Wan2.2 (Alibaba, July 2025) | The book mentions Wan2.1 (covered by instructions). Wan2.2 is a follow-on that introduced a Mixture-of-Experts architecture (27B parameters, 14B active), 720P at 24fps, and Apache 2.0 licensing, released as the world's first open-source MoE video generation model. The book's video generation landscape section (36.2) would benefit from noting this successor. | Section 36.2 open-model ecosystem subsection |
| 35 (controllable) | FLUX.1 Kontext (May 2025) for in-context image editing | Section 35.1-35.6 covers ControlNet, IP-Adapter, DreamBooth, LoRA, and image editing. FLUX.1 Kontext enables reference-image-conditioned editing (change style while preserving identity, re-contextualize subjects) in a single model without requiring separate ControlNet adapter training. This is a new editing paradigm not covered in chapter 35. | Section 35.6 or as a frontier callout in section 35.3 |
